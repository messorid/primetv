The TCL QM5K carries the lowest number in TCL's QM range and is one of the heaviest installations we do. It is sold at **85 inches only**.

That combination catches people out. The model number suggests an entry-level set; the box on the living room floor is the size of a door. If you have one of these waiting to go up, this guide is the order we would do it in.

---

## Start Here: This Is a Wall Project, Not a TV Project

Every smaller install is a question about the television. At 85 inches it is a question about your house.

**An 85-inch panel typically weighs 90 lbs or more**, and that weight hangs off whatever is behind your drywall for as long as you own the set. So the first real task is not the panel. It is the framing.

Drywall on its own cannot hold this. Not with anchors, not with toggles, not with anything sold as heavy duty. What holds an 85-inch television is the **timber behind the drywall**, and the bracket has to be lagged into it.

The failure mode is worth understanding, because it is why we are so insistent. A badly anchored 85-inch mount does not fail immediately or gracefully. It holds for months. Then it lets go, and it takes a section of wall, a very large piece of glass, and usually the console underneath it. We are called out to re-anchor other people's work at this size more than at any other.

---

## Step 1: Find the Studs, Then Prove Them

Mark every stud finder hit across the area. Then **confirm** each mark — a second pass from the opposite direction, a magnet tracking the line of drywall screws, or both.

Two confirmed studs is the absolute minimum. Three is better and, on standard 16-inch framing, an 85-inch bracket can usually reach three.

At this size we will occasionally open a small inspection point rather than guess, because the cost of being wrong is a wall repair plus a destroyed television. "Probably a stud" is not good enough for 90 lbs.

If the wall is masonry, plaster over lath, or a metal-stud partition, it can still take the TV, but the hardware and the approach change. Surcharge for concrete, tile, stone or metal starts at **$25**; drywall carries no extra charge.

---

## Step 2: Two People. There Is No Alternative.

An 85-inch QM5K is far wider than one person's reach and heavier than most people expect from a set at this price.

There is no version of this install that one person should attempt, and we will not send one technician regardless of how the job is quoted. One takes the weight, one guides the panel onto the rail and confirms it has seated.

If you are doing this yourself, three people is not excessive — two on the panel, one calling the alignment.

---

## Step 3: Height — Lower Than the Formula Says

The usual advice is centre-of-screen at seated eye level, 42 to 48 inches off the floor. That advice was written for smaller televisions.

Apply it literally to an 85-inch screen and the bottom edge can end up **behind your media console**. The rule still points in the right direction, but at this size it stops being arithmetic and becomes a judgement.

In practice the honest answer is usually **the lowest height your furniture allows**. Put the console where it will live, sit on the sofa, and find the point where the bottom of the screen clears everything without the top of the screen forcing your head back. Mark from there.

---

## Step 4: Bracket, Fitted and Checked

Attach the bracket arms to the panel and confirm they sit flat and that their spacing lands on the studs you marked.

Use a **heavy-duty bracket rated for the panel**, lagged into timber. Full-motion arms exist at 85 inches, but they multiply the leverage on the anchors considerably, and we only fit one where the framing genuinely supports it. If you want the screen to move, say so early — it changes the wall requirement, not just the hardware.

The QM series uses QD-Mini LED backlighting, so the chassis is deeper than a thin edge-lit panel. If you want the TV close to the wall, a slim fixed mount is the answer and a full-motion arm is not.

---

## Step 5: Cables, Because There Are More of Them

At 85 inches there is almost always a soundbar, a console and a streaming box to route, and a visible bundle of cables under a screen this size is impossible to ignore.

Plan the route before the bracket is fixed if you want in-wall concealment. It starts **from $60 per TV**; a paintable surface raceway is the fallback where the wall cannot be opened.

## A Note on the Price of the TV

The QM5K has made 85 inches genuinely affordable, and that is a good thing.

It does not make the installation cheaper. The wall work, the crew and the bracket are identical whether the panel cost six hundred dollars or four thousand. We mention it because the temptation to cut corners is strongest exactly when the TV was a bargain, and the wall has no idea what you paid.

## What About Above the Fireplace?

At 85 inches we will often advise against it, and we would rather say so before you book.

Above a typical Nashville mantel a screen this large either sits inside the heat plume from the firebox or sits so high that the viewing angle spoils the room — frequently both. Over-fireplace handling starts **from $25 extra** where the geometry works. Where it does not, we will suggest a different wall.
