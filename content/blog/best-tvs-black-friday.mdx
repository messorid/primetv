### LG C6 — the OLED most people should buy

The C6 is LG's mid-range OLED for 2026, with sizes from 42 to 83 inches and a 165Hz panel for gaming. At launch the 55-inch was $1,999 and the 65-inch $2,699, but the 65-inch was already selling for $1,399.99 on Amazon in September. If you see the 65-inch well under that September price on Black Friday, it's a genuinely good buy.

Note that the 77 and 83-inch versions are sold as the **C6H** and use LG's brighter top-tier panel.

**On the wall:** the 65-inch weighs about 37 lbs without the stand and uses a 300 x 200 VESA pattern, so most mid-size brackets fit. We wrote a full guide to [mounting the 65-inch LG C6](/blog/how-to-mount-lg-oled-c6-65-inch).

### LG B6 — the cheapest good OLED

The B6 is LG's entry OLED, with a 120Hz panel instead of 165Hz. The 65-inch lists at $1,999.99 and has already hit $1,119.99, a price TechRadar's team called a record low for what it rates as the best budget OLED of 2026.

At Best Buy you'll also see the **B6E**, a store-only version. In RTINGS' testing it performs the same as the B6, but it comes with a simpler remote instead of LG's Magic Remote and drops a couple of picture and sound features. At $999.99 for the 65-inch it's a fair trade for most people — just know it's a different model number, so other stores can't price-match it.

### Samsung S95H — for bright rooms

Samsung's 2026 flagship is a QD-OLED with a **matte, glare-free screen**, which is what you want facing a window. It runs 55 to 83 inches; the 65-inch launched at $3,399.99. In the US the S95H is the model the UK calls the S99H, so don't go by European reviews of the "S95H" — that's a different TV.

Its ports are on the TV itself. Samsung sells a Wireless One Connect box separately (about $350 at launch) if you want only a power cord running to the wall.

### Hisense U7SG — the most TV for the money

The U7SG is Hisense's top standard Mini-LED for 2026 (only its pricier RGB models sit above it), from 55 to 116 inches, with a 165Hz panel and four HDMI 2.1 ports. The 65-inch launched at $1,499.99 and has sold for $949.99 on Amazon. It's the one to look at if you want 75 inches or more without paying OLED prices.

**On the wall:** the 65-inch is about 43 lbs with a 400 x 300 VESA pattern. Our [Hisense TV mounting page](/hisense-tv-mounting) covers the bracket sizes for each screen.

### TCL QM7L — big screens on a budget

TCL's 2026 QM7L Mini-LED comes in 55 to 98 inches; launch prices ran from $1,199.99 for the 55-inch to $2,499.99 for the 85-inch. The trade-off against the Hisense: it has **two HDMI 2.1 ports instead of four**, which matters only if you connect more than two current game consoles or a gaming PC.

**On the wall:** the 85-inch weighs roughly 72 to 75 lbs with a 600 x 400 VESA pattern — two-person territory. See our [85-inch TV mounting page](/85-inch-tv-mounting) before you order a bracket.

### Samsung The Frame and Frame Pro — for a TV that looks like art

The 2026 **Frame** (55 to 98 inches; 65-inch $1,499.99) keeps its ports on the TV. The **Frame Pro** steps up to Mini-LED and includes Samsung's Wireless One Connect box and a slim wall mount; its 65-inch launched at $2,000 and sold for $1,597.99 during Amazon's October sale.

## How to tell a real Black Friday deal

1. **Compare against this fall's prices, not the launch price.** A "$1,300 off" sticker means little if the TV already sold for that in September. The prices in the table above are a good baseline.
2. **Check the exact model number.** Store-only versions like the LG B6E differ by a letter and sometimes by features. They can still be great value — just compare like with like.
3. **Look for the native refresh rate.** "Motion Rate 240" usually means a 120Hz panel, and "Motion Rate 120" a 60Hz one. The spec sheet lists the real figure.
4. **Count the HDMI 2.1 ports** if you game. Four is better than two when you have several consoles.
5. **Read the return window before you buy.** Holiday return periods vary by store and change every year.

## Before the TV goes on the wall

A new TV is the easy part. Here's what decides whether it hangs safely:

- **Weight.** A 65-inch TV is roughly 35 to 50 lbs; a 75 to 85-inch TV is 50 to 100 lbs. Sony's 85-inch Bravia 7 II, for example, is 101 lbs without the stand.
- **The mount's rating.** Choose a bracket rated comfortably above the TV's weight and matching its VESA pattern (listed in the manual).
- **Your wall.** Drywall over wood studs is the simple case. Brick, stone, tile and concrete need different anchors — see [mounting a TV over a fireplace](/blog/can-you-mount-tv-over-fireplace) if that's where it's going.
- **Cables.** Decide now whether you want the cords hidden inside the wall. It's much easier to do on installation day than afterwards — here's [how to hide TV wires](/blog/how-to-hide-tv-wires-nashville).
- **Thin screens.** OLEDs are very thin and flexible at the edges; lift and hang them with two people, holding the frame rather than the panel.
