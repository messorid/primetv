Most television series hedge their bets. They launch at 43 inches for the bedroom crowd, run up through 55 and 65 for the living room, and top out somewhere large for people with a wall to fill.

The Samsung U8000F does not do that. It is sold in **75 and 85 inches only**. There is no small one. Which means something useful: if you own a U8000F, you own a large-format television, and every piece of advice that begins "you can probably manage this on your own" does not apply to you.

---

## The U8000F Versus the U8000H

We get asked this a lot, so let us deal with it early.

From a picture and features point of view there are differences between the U8000F and the [U8000H](/blog/how-to-mount-samsung-u8000h-75-inch), and Samsung will happily explain them. From a **mounting** point of view there are essentially none. Both are Crystal UHD LED panels. Both are thin. Both hang off a conventional bracket lagged into studs.

The only difference that changes our day is the one already mentioned: the U8000H is also sold at 65 inches, where a single technician can often handle it. The U8000F never is.

---

## Step 1: Establish What the Wall Is Made Of

Before the studs, the wall itself. Nashville housing stock is varied enough that this is a real question.

Standard drywall over timber framing is the easy case and the most common one. But we also work in older homes with **plaster over lath**, in condo conversions with **metal stud partitions**, and against **masonry chimney breasts**. All three will hold a 75-inch U8000F perfectly well. All three need different hardware from the drywall case, and two of them need a slower, more careful approach.

Knock on the wall. A hollow, drummy sound with solid points every 16 inches is timber framing. A dense, unforgiving sound is masonry. If you are not sure, that is a good reason to have someone look at it.

Our surcharge for concrete, tile, stone or metal starts at **$25**. Drywall carries no extra charge.

## Step 2: Find and Confirm Two Studs

On timber framing, mark every stud across the area where the TV is going, then verify each mark rather than trusting the first beep.

The target is a bracket **centred across two studs**. On 16-inch centres a 75-inch mount frequently reaches three, and a third is always welcome on a panel this size.

What you cannot do — and we want to be unambiguous about this because we are called out to fix it regularly — is hang a 75-inch television on drywall anchors, or on a single stud with anchors either side. It will appear to work. It will hold for months. Then it will not, and it will take the drywall, the panel and whatever is underneath with it.

---

## Step 3: Get a Second Person Before You Get a Drill

A 75-inch U8000F is wider than one person can reach across. The failure is not usually dropping it; it is the moment someone is holding the full weight with both arms and trying to feel for the bracket hooks blind, at head height, with no hand free.

Two people. One takes the weight, one guides the hooks onto the rail and confirms the seat. Thirty seconds, no drama.

We put two technicians on every U8000F install, at both sizes, and it is not an upsell — it is the only way we do it.

---

## Step 4: Height, Decided From the Sofa

Centre of the screen near seated eye level, which generally lands between 42 and 48 inches from the floor.

At 75 inches you have to sanity-check that against the room rather than just measuring it out. The screen is tall enough that a textbook centre height can push the bottom edge down to where a soundbar or console gets in the way. Put the furniture in place first, sit down, hold a marker on the wall, and look at it properly before committing.

---

## Step 5: Cables, Then Test Everything

Decide on cable routing before the bracket goes up, because in-wall routing depends on where the TV finally lands. In-wall gives an invisible finish; a painted surface raceway is the honest fallback where the wall will not open. Concealment starts **from $60 per TV**.

Then actually test it. Power on, cycle every input, confirm the soundbar and the console genuinely pass through the set rather than assuming they will. A television that is mounted but not working is not a finished job.
