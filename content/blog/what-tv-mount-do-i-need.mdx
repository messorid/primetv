The right TV mount is the one that matches **your TV's hole pattern and weight, your wall, and how you watch**. Get those three right and almost any well-made mount will do the job; get one wrong and the mount either won't fit or won't be safe. Here is how to choose in about five minutes.

<BlackFridayNote />

## Key takeaways

- **Three numbers decide whether a mount fits**: VESA pattern, TV weight and screen size.
- **Fixed** for straight-on viewing, **tilting** when the TV sits a little high, **full-motion** for corners and more than one viewing spot.
- **Bigger TVs need the mount to reach two studs**, especially full-motion mounts.
- **The wall changes the hardware**: drywall over wood studs is standard; brick, stone, tile and metal studs are not.
- **We don't sell mounts**, but we'll check your choice before you order it.

## Start with the TV: three numbers

Look these up for your exact model, in the manual or on the manufacturer's page.

**1. VESA pattern.** The four threaded holes on the back of the TV form a rectangle. Its size in millimeters, width × height, is the VESA pattern — 200 × 200, 300 × 300, 400 × 400 and 600 × 400 are common ones. The mount must list your pattern.

**2. Weight without the stand.** Every mount has a maximum weight. Choose one rated comfortably above your TV's weight, not right at the limit.

**3. Screen size.** Mounts are sold for a range of sizes, such as 37 to 75 inches. Size is the loosest of the three — the VESA pattern and the weight are what really decide the fit.

A simple way to decide: **if everyone watches from the same sofa, buy fixed or tilting.** If the TV has to face the kitchen some days and the sofa on others, buy full-motion.

## What changes with the size of the TV

**Up to 55 inches.** The easiest case. These TVs are light enough that most mounts of any type work, and one or two studs are enough.

**65 inches.** Any type works, but a full-motion mount should have a wall plate wide enough to bolt into two studs.

**75 inches.** Weight and width start to matter. Look for a mount rated well above the TV's weight, with a wide wall plate. Fixed and tilting mounts are the simplest choice at this size.

**85 inches and up.** Choose the mount for the weight first. Fixed or tilting is what we recommend unless you truly need the TV to move, and it takes two people to hang. If you want full-motion at this size, buy a heavy-duty model built for it.

## Now check the wall

The hardware in the box is almost always for **wood studs behind drywall**, usually spaced 16 inches apart. That is the standard install.

Other walls need a different plan:

- **Brick, stone or concrete** — masonry anchors instead of wood screws. See [TV mounting on brick, stone or tile](/blog/tv-mounting-brick-stone-tile).
- **Tile** — drilled with care so it doesn't crack, then anchored into what is behind it.
- **Metal studs** — common in apartments and condos; they need anchors made for them.
- **Above a fireplace** — a tilting or pull-down mount, after a heat and height check. See [can you mount a TV over a fireplace](/blog/can-you-mount-tv-over-fireplace).

Drywall anchors alone, with no stud, are not enough for a TV of any real size.
